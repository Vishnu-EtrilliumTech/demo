AI Executive Summary Preview

Mehra Textiles Pvt. Ltd. has moved the Commercial Division of the Delhi High Court to restrain the State Bank of India from classifying its loan account as a non-performing asset and from initiating recovery under the SARFAESI Act, pending adjudication of a disputed 2023 restructuring agreement.

An interim injunction granted in January 2026 has held throughout and was continued on 11 June 2026. The matter is presently at the framing-of-issues stage, listed for 17 July 2026.

Plain-language summary for litigants

The company says the bank agreed to restructure its loan in 2023 and cannot now treat the account as defaulted. Until the court decides whether that agreement is binding, the bank has been told not to take recovery action. The next step is for the court to define the exact questions it will decide.

Legal issues

Primary issue

Whether the 2023 restructuring letter constitutes a binding, concluded agreement that estops SBI from classifying the account as an NPA and enforcing recovery.

Secondary issues

Whether interim protection ought to continue pending trial; and whether the dispute is arbitrable given the facility agreement's arbitration clause.

Statutes cited & applied

SARFAESI Act, 2002 § 13(2), 13(4)
Recovery mechanism whose invocation is sought to be restrained pending adjudication.
Specific Relief Act, 1963 § 41
Governs grant of injunctive relief; respondent argues bar on restraining statutory action.
Indian Contract Act, 1872 § 25, 62
Relied on by petitioner for novation via the restructuring letter.

Arguments & reasoning

Petitioner

The 2023 restructuring letter novated the original facility; the bank accepted revised instalments, and is estopped from reclassifying the account. Recovery before adjudication would cause irreparable harm to a going concern.

Respondent

The letter was a without-prejudice proposal that lapsed for non-compliance. Statutory recovery under SARFAESI cannot be injuncted, and the balance of convenience favours the secured creditor.

Court's reasoning

The Court found a triable issue on whether the restructuring was concluded, and held the balance of convenience favoured preserving status quo given the disputed factual matrix around instalment acceptance.
